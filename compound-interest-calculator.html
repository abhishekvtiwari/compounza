Understanding Compound Interest

Compound interest is interest calculated on both the initial principal and the accumulated interest from previous periods. Unlike simple interest, compounding causes your wealth to grow exponentially.

A = P × (1 + r/n)^(n×t)
CI = A − P

Where: P = Principal, r = Annual rate (decimal), n = Compounding frequency per year, t = Time in years

The Effective Annual Rate (EAR) tells you the real annual return after accounting for compounding frequency. More frequent compounding always results in a higher EAR than the nominal rate.

The Rule of 72 is a quick mental shortcut: divide 72 by the annual rate to estimate how many years it takes for your money to double.
